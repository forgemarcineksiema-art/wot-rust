# The first battles actually played — 2026-08-01

Until this session nobody had measured how a battle in this game **runs**, only how it is built.
Real 7v7s were driven from a scripted input sequence through the full timer:
`LocalAuthoritativeServer::new_random_7v7`, seed 7, Bystra Valley, player in a T-54.

Harness: `crates/apps/client/examples/play_session.rs` (temporary — see `README.md`).

**Read the correction first.** The first pass produced three dramatic findings and all three were
wrong, from one habit. What follows is the corrected record, kept in full because the failure is
more instructive than the findings would have been.

---

## The correction — three headline findings, all mine, all wrong

| claimed | measured |
|---|---|
| **"Zero ricochets in 53 hits — the core promise never fires"** | **8 ricochets in 79 impacts (~10 %).** Of the 10 impacts above the 70° threshold, **8 ricocheted**; the other two are overmatch, which correctly disables ricochet. |
| **"A 7v7 does not resolve inside its own 7-minute timer"** | The limit is **600 s, not 420** — I invented the seven minutes. The battle ends exactly on the limit. |
| **"Thirteen bots killed nobody in a minute — the bots barely fight"** | **79 impacts and 9 of 14 tanks destroyed** across the battle. |

**One root cause for all three.** The harness logged only events with `damage_hp > 0` — and a
ricochet deals zero damage by definition, so every bounce was filtered out by the instrument before
I ever saw it. The "passive bots" reading came from the same truncated log. The timer claim came
from assuming a constant instead of reading `RANDOM_BATTLE_TIME_LIMIT_S`.

**The lesson, recorded on purpose:** this is exactly the failure this program documents elsewhere —
*"a contract nobody runs on the real thing is a document, not a gate."* An instrument that has not
been checked against the thing it measures is not evidence. Verify the tool before trusting the
number, especially when the number is dramatic.

Angles spread across the whole range rather than clustering flat, which is what "the bots only ever
shoot perpendicular" would have looked like. The armour model is doing its job.

## The finding that survived

**A 4-to-1 battle is declared a draw.** `Draw { reason: TimeExpired }` with a fourfold tank
advantage on the board is the least satisfying resolution available. Most games in this genre award
the win on tanks remaining or damage dealt when the clock runs out. This is a rule decision, cheap
to change, and it is the one thing the playtest turned up that is genuinely broken.

## Also observed

**The camera freezes when the player dies.** Four rendered frames from t5042 to t20162 are
pixel-identical while the battle continues around them. `tick_death_spectate` exists in
`app/camera_link.rs` but does not drive this path.

## Muzzle-flash latency — the one claim that held up under measurement

The flash is derived from a jump in `reload_remaining_s` between snapshots (`fx/fire.rs:1-5`), and I
first claimed that meant "≤50 ms from the 20 Hz cadence". Measured over seven shots, zero variance:
**1 tick = 16.7 ms.** The local snapshot lands on the firing tick, so in local play this is
imperceptible and the priority of predicting the shot drops accordingly.

What stands: the cue is DERIVED rather than PREDICTED, so networked play adds RTT on top; and
`fire.rs:27` requires `latest.hit_points > 0`, so a tank that fires and dies inside one snapshot
window shows no muzzle flash at all.
